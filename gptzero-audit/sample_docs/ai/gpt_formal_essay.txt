Social media has profoundly transformed the landscape of democracy, acting both as a catalyst for political engagement and a challenge to democratic norms. On one hand, platforms like Twitter, Facebook, and Instagram have democratized information dissemination, enabling citizens to share ideas, mobilize for causes, and engage in political discourse like never before. Grassroots movements, such as the Arab Spring and Black Lives Matter, have leveraged social media to amplify their messages and organize protests, highlighting its power as a tool for democratic activism.

However, the impact of social media on democracy is not unequivocally positive. The spread of misinformation and disinformation poses significant threats to informed decision-making, often leading to polarization and undermining trust in institutions. Algorithms that prioritize sensational content can create echo chambers, where users are exposed primarily to ideas that reinforce their existing beliefs. This fragmentation of the public sphere can impede constructive dialogue and compromise the deliberative nature essential for a healthy democracy.

Moreover, the influence of foreign actors on social media platforms raises concerns about external interference in democratic processes. Ultimately, while social media has the potential to enhance democratic engagement, it also necessitates careful consideration and regulation to mitigate its risks and promote a more informed and cohesive public discourse.

**Empty Collar**

The house feels impossibly quiet now. I keep expecting to hear the familiar jingle of Max's collar or the soft padding of his paws across the kitchen floor. It's been three weeks since we said goodbye, and I still find myself saving the last bite of my sandwich for him.

Twelve years. That's how long his tail wagged at the sound of my key in the door. Twelve years of morning walks, belly rubs, and those deep brown eyes that somehow always knew when I'd had a rough day. He was more than a pet—he was my shadow, my confidant, my constant.

The vet said it was time, that keeping him longer would be selfish. I held him as he drifted away, whispering "good boy" through my tears. God, I miss him.

I thought I was prepared for this. You know it's coming eventually. But nothing prepares you for the absence—how your whole routine revolves around something that's suddenly gone. The tennis ball still sits by the door. I can't bring myself to move it yet.

People say it gets easier. I hope they're right. But right now, I'd give anything for one more walk, one more gentle nudge of his wet nose against my hand.

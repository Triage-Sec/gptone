**The Keeper's Discovery**

Edmund had tended Beacon Point Light for thirty-seven years, watching ships pass like distant memories on the horizon. His weathered hands moved through their daily routine—polishing the lens, trimming the wick, checking the oil—until the storm changed everything.

The nor'easter battered the coast for three days, hurling waves against the lighthouse's stone foundation. When the sea finally calmed, Edmund descended to inspect for damage. Among the scattered kelp and driftwood, a green bottle caught his eye, its cork still intact despite the battering.

Inside, a child's careful handwriting covered a piece of notebook paper: *"My name is Sarah. I'm eight. Daddy says you keep the light burning for sailors like him. He's been gone six months. If you find this, please keep the light extra bright tonight. Maybe he'll see it and come home. Thank you for watching over him."*

Edmund's throat tightened. He'd never married, never had children, yet this little girl's faith pierced through decades of solitude. That evening, he climbed the spiral stairs with unusual urgency. He polished the lens until it gleamed, adjusted the reflectors, and added extra oil.

As darkness fell, Beacon Point Light blazed brighter than it had in years. Edmund stood watch until dawn, scanning the horizon. Just before sunrise, a fishing vessel appeared, its captain waving gratefully toward the tower.

Edmund never knew if it was Sarah's father, but it didn't matter. From that night forward, the light burned brighter—not just for ships, but for hope itself.
